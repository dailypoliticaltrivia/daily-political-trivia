Corrections and data

How the games' questions and answers are built, and what to do if one is wrong.

Last updated: November 15, 2026

How the games are built

  • Congressional Grid: each row and column is a fact about members of Congress (for example a state, party, committee, chamber or a first or last name). A name is correct if the member's recorded data fits both labels. We work out the labels from the congressional records described below.
  • Dark Horse: seven questions a day from question banks written for this site, covering civics and public officeholders. You type an answer; rarer correct answers score more, and some questions hide a bonus answer called the Dark Horse. Each question has a short factual note with the answer.
  • Caucus Sort: sixteen portraits to sort into four groups, built from recorded facts such as office, state, party or service dates.
  • Capitol Climb: multiple-choice questions on American history, written for this site from public records and standard references. Each answer has a short factual note.

Where the data comes from

  • Congress members, 1789 to today, committees and dates: the Biographical Directory of the United States Congress and the unitedstates project.
  • Facts about people, offices and dates: Wikidata (CC0), and public-domain works of the U.S. government.
  • Election results used for newly elected officials: official state sources and major news organisations, checked by hand against two or more sources. People elected on 3 November 2026 who have not yet taken office are never counted as having served.
  • Photographs and the question banks: see below and the Credits page.

The games use factual public records only. They do not publish opinion, commentary or user content, and we do not endorse any candidate, party or cause.

How photos are credited

Portraits come from official U.S. government sources (public domain) and from Wikimedia Commons under public-domain or Creative Commons licences. Every photo that needs a credit is listed individually on the Credits page with the photographer or author, the licence and a link to the original. We only crop and resize photos. If a credit is wrong or missing, or you own a photo and want it removed, write to us (see below) or use our copyright page.

How often the data refreshes

The congressional roster data was last refreshed on 10 November 2026, and we update it when membership changes and after elections. A new Congress takes office in January, and official portraits of new members usually appear after swearing-in, so some new members have no photograph until then. The games reflect the data as of the last refresh, so a very recent change (a resignation, death or appointment) may not appear yet.

Report a wrong answer

Mistakes happen, especially with historical records and changing offices. If you think a question, an answer, a label or a photo is wrong, write to corrections@dailypoiticaltrivia.com and tell us:

  • which game and which day (or the page address);
  • what you think is wrong;
  • a source, if you have one (an official page is best).

We check it against at least two sources where we can. We correct factual mistakes promptly and, for a significant one, add a note saying what changed and when. We will not change an answer because someone asks us to favour or disfavour a person, only because it is wrong.

About the people shown

The games name and show real public officials using factual public records, in an educational context. If you are shown in a game and want to correct a fact or ask for a photo to be removed or replaced, write to corrections@dailypoiticaltrivia.com. See the Terms of Use.
