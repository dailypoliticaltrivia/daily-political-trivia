Daily Political Trivia

Daily Political Trivia is a free place to build your political knowledge, test it and compete with your friends. Study with the Congress Map, then play four daily games about Congress, politics and American history.

Learn with the map

The Congress Map is the study guide for the games. Pick any year since 1789, click a state and then a district, and see who held the seat: the House member, both senators and the governor, with their party, dates and career. Look up your own representatives by ZIP code or town, take a guided tour, or watch district lines move with each census and redistricting.

The games

Here they are from easiest to hardest. Capitol Climb sends you along twenty stops around Washington, DC, with a U.S. history question at each one and three hearts to spare. Caucus Sort asks you to sort 16 politician faces into four hidden groups, with an easy mode that shows you the four categories. Dark Horse gives you seven questions and 30 seconds each: type your answer, and the rarer it is, the more it scores. Congressional Grid asks you to name a member of Congress who fits both a row and a column. Each game has one puzzle a day, the same for everyone, plus unlimited practice rounds.

Compete with friends

Everyone gets the same puzzles each day, so you can compare results directly. Every result shares as a small grid of squares that shows how you did without spoiling the answers. Send it to friends, family or your group chat and see who knows their politics best.

How scoring works

Rarer answers score more. A member from a small state, or an answer few people know, is worth more than the obvious one. Results copy as a small grid of squares that gives nothing away.
