The four games, easiest to hardest

Capitol Climb

Travel the National Mall and answer U.S. history questions at the places where it happened. Everyone gets the same route today.

Difficulty: Easiest

Scoring
Twenty stops and three hearts. A wrong answer costs a heart.
Time
No timer.
Modes
The daily journey, or Explore DC to pick any stop and keep going.
Play Capitol Climb

Caucus Sort

Sixteen politician faces hide four groups of four. Pick four that belong together, then submit.

Difficulty: Medium

Scoring
No points. Find all four groups with no more than four mistakes.
Time
No timer.
Modes
Daily or practice puzzles, an easy mode that shows the four categories, and a faces-only mode with the names hidden.
Play Caucus Sort

Dark Horse

Seven questions about politics and public office. Type an answer before the clock runs out. Last names count, and a misspelling gets a “Did you mean?” to confirm.

Difficulty: Hard

Scoring
The rarer your correct answer, the more it scores: 5, 20, 45, 70 or 100 points.
Time
30 seconds a question, so 3½ minutes at most.
Modes
The daily round, plus unlimited practice rounds.
Play Dark Horse

Congressional Grid

A three-by-three grid with a category on each row and column. Name a member of Congress who fits both labels for each square.

Difficulty: Hardest

Scoring
Nine guesses. Each correct pick gets a rarity score from 1 to 100, and a perfect grid is 9. Lowest total wins.
Time
No timer.
Modes
Congress or Presidents, plus unlimited practice grids.
Play Congressional Grid

How it works

  • 1. Learn. Explore the Congress Map: who holds every seat today and who held it in any year since 1789, with photos, parties and careers.
  • 2. Test yourself. Four games, one fresh puzzle each at midnight, the same for every player. Rarer answers score more, so knowing the less obvious names pays off. Practice rounds are unlimited.
  • 3. Compete with friends. Every result shares as a small spoiler-free grid of squares. Send it to your group chat and see who knows their politics best.

Questions

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free and there is nothing to sign up for. Your progress for the day is saved in your own browser.

Are there ads?

No. The site has no advertising, sponsors or affiliate links right now.

When does a new puzzle appear?

At midnight, your local time. Everyone sees the same daily puzzle, so you can compare results with friends.

Where do the facts come from?

Congress data comes from the Biographical Directory of the United States Congress and the unitedstates project. Facts about people and offices come from Wikidata and U.S. government public records. The Corrections and data page lists every source.

I think an answer is wrong. What do I do?

Tell us. The Corrections and data page explains what to send, and we check it against at least two sources.
